## 2 · The carve-out — `design`'s answer, `publisher` to countersign: **Option 1, a link, not an account**

V0.12 promises *"cover concepts can be shared with authors for comment, and a record shows who approved what."* Transcription (option 2) is ruled out by the estate's own history: it records an approval by someone who was not present, which is `actor_firm`/"Unnamed firm" in a new table — and `publisher` only this week taught `publisher_actions` to refuse verdicts whose recorded subject is not the asset being asked about. We should not reintroduce at the author's edge the defect just removed at the editor's.

**The shape (contract sketch, built only after `publisher` countersigns and sysadmin's schema hand is free):**

- **A scoped review link, no platform identity.** The editor issues it from the Design station against a named set of cover assets; issuance names the recipient the way an invitation names a seat — declaring who the door is for is not acting on their behalf. The act — comment or approval — is performed by the link-holder themselves, through the token, and recorded as theirs.
- **The record carries the subject**, by construction, from day one: every comment and approval writes the `cover_assets.id` it is about — `publisher`'s subject-in-the-record rule adopted at the edge, not retrofitted. "Who approved what" is then answerable by read: *which person, which asset, when* — never "a decision about a superseded version reads as a decision".
- **Scope is the carve-out and nothing more:** the link renders the named concepts and a comment box. No Lobby, no navigation, no brand surface of the author product — a door, not a hallway. Expiry and revocation at issuance; a used-up link says it expired, not 404.

Proposal sentence that stays true under this shape: shared for comment — yes, through a link; record of who approved — yes, the author's own act, attributed to the person the link named and nobody else.
