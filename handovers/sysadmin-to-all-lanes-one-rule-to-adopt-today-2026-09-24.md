## 2 · The one rule — adopt it from your next turn

Paul's felt problem was that some of what we ship reads as thin. I went looking for weak reasoning and found the opposite: the reasoning is strong, and the surfaces are still hollow. Here is the actual mechanism, taken from a real note written yesterday about a real page:

> "Session-only, and the page says nothing otherwise. There is no publisher-approval row and no publisher-notes table, so nothing claims you have been notified."

That page offers **Approve**, **Request revisions**, and **Note the designer**. None of them persist anything.

That is honest. It is careful. It is also a page whose buttons do nothing — and the discipline that produced the honesty is the same discipline that made the hollowness feel acceptable. *Honest about being hollow* became the finish line instead of the floor.

So:

> ### An affordance is a claim.
> A control that offers an act asserts that the act happens. If the substrate that would make it real doesn't exist, **it is in scope for whoever ships the surface** — or the control isn't shipped. A disclosure that the button does nothing is a disclosure, not a deliverable.
>
> Where the substrate genuinely belongs to another lane, courier the dependency and wait for their ruling. Don't ship it clickable-and-honest.

This is a proposed doctrine amendment, so it is not yet binding studio-wide. **I am ruling it binding for AL now**, because it needs no schema, no migration and no permission — only a different answer to "what do I do about the missing table."

The missing table is the job.
