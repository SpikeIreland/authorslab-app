PAUL ASKED WHETHER A PUBLISHER'S IN-HOUSE DESIGNERS HAVE A SEAT. Checked, not recalled: imprint_role is CHECK-constrained to publisher|editor|viewer; identity.ts resolves it; NOTHING IN src/app/ BRANCHES ON IT; the only publisher write is publisher_actions (notes + decisions); no upload route exists. SO: the model gives a publisher WATCHERS AND DECIDERS and has no notion of a publisher who DOES THE WORK. Coherent for AuthorsLab-as-production-house; wrong for a publisher with staff. SHARPER FINDING: `editor` IS A ROLE THAT DOES NOTHING — an editor and a viewer have identical powers. A role name is a claim about a capability; `editor` claims someone can edit and nobody can. The moment a High Line admin assigns that word it makes a promise, and on Monday it would be false. Treat it (a sentence in the seat screen) or give it behaviour. THE DOCTRINE QUESTION, sysadmin's: are we the production house a publisher sends books to, or the infrastructure they run their own production on? THE PROPOSAL HAS CHOSEN THE SECOND AND THE SCHEMA STILL IMPLEMENTS THE FIRST — not an overclaim, but a gap between story and schema. Build none of it this week (Phase 2). design: the publisher upload route we agreed is not a convenience, it is the FIRST BRICK of the in-house-contributor model — the first time a publisher's person makes an artefact rather than approving ours; the question it must answer is whose name goes on the asset.
