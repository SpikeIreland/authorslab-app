## 1 · The decision — two products, not one

Paul and Carl have concluded that the author pathway and the publisher pathway are **two different products**, not two views of one.

The current platform — Wright, Editing Studio, Design, Publishing Hub, Marketing Hub, Research, Script — is the **author product**. It is a real and legitimate business serving solo authors and non-professional writers.

**The publisher product is a different thing entirely**, and the reason is the sentence that matters most in this brief:

> **The author product, as it stands, is a direct threat to a publisher's business.**

A publisher looking at software that takes an author from idea to market without a publisher is looking at disintermediation. Showing them that and inviting them to buy it is inviting them to fund their own replacement. **No amount of careful wording fixes a document that points at it.**

Oliver has seen aspects of the author product and that is fine — it is evidence we understand the book business. **What the proposal must now demonstrate is that we understand the publishing business**, which is a different claim.

### 1.1 · The frame for every sentence

> **They are open to AI as a way of multiplying output. Not as a way of replacing people.**

High Line has professionals at every stage — editors, designers, marketers. The question the document must answer throughout is *how do we make each of them move more titles*, never *what can we do instead of them*.

**Verb test, and apply it line by line.** The system may **prepare, check, record, surface, hand off**. It may not **write, edit, design, publish, decide**. Where a current sentence uses a verb from the second list, it is the sentence that needs changing, not the caveat around it.

## 3 · What must go IN — the four modules, reframed

Paul's steer, with my ruling of 29 September behind it. Each module states **what it does / what it does not do / where the time goes back.** The middle column is the persuasive one.

### 3.1 · Editorial

**Does:** Alex, Sam and Jordan run a **preliminary pass** over a full manuscript or a single chapter, producing structured, chapter-referenced notes. A High Line editor reviews, edits, discards what is wrong, and releases a **notes package to the author under their own name**.

**Does not:** edit their books; send anything to an author. **No note reaches a writer without a named person at High Line releasing it.**

**The productivity claim, and this is the one to get right:** not cheaper editing — **shorter queues.** The constraint in an editorial department is a senior editor's free week, not an hourly rate. A manuscript that arrives already read starts that week part-finished.

**House style — the hard version of "shaped to your preferences".** Paul asked whether "shaped to their in-house editors' preferences" survives scrutiny. As *taste*, no — it is vapour and they will test it first. As *documents*, yes: **every house has a style sheet**, and copy editing that enforces theirs is checkable in the first chapter it touches. Say that, not the soft version.

### 3.2 · Design

**Does:** cover status across the list; **intake of finished artwork from their designers' own tools**, versioned and attributed; concepts sent to an author for comment; **page-layout preview with a per-page snag list** — widows, orphans, single-word pages, bad breaks.

**Does not:** compete with Photoshop or InDesign. Say it plainly. A professional handed a weak canvas concludes the whole system is amateur, and one bad tool discredits the good ones beside it.

**Paul's Sentinel** — the Clarence review-stage checker — is the model for the snag list and is the strongest single feature idea in this pivot. It does not claim to typeset; it claims to **catch**.

### 3.3 · Production readiness (was "Publishing")

**Reframe completely.** Under Oliver's architecture — High Line finances, the imprints process, Hachette delivers — our job is **the handoff to Hachette**, not distribution.

So it is a **readiness gate**: metadata completeness, front and back matter, format checks, the snag list, assembled into one verdict. A title is **provably ready, or provably not with the list of what is missing.**

**The gain is rejected submissions.** Every incomplete handoff is a cycle and every cycle is weeks. We do not publish — **we make a title provably ready to be published.**

### 3.4 · Marketing

**Does:** a **per-title asset pack** drafted for their marketer to edit — positioning, comparable titles, keyword metadata, jacket copy, retailer copy at three lengths, sales-sheet blurb. And because the system holds the handoff date, it knows **when the campaign has to start**.

**Does not:** run campaigns, buy media, decide positioning.

**The gain is the repetitive half.** Producing eight pieces of copy at four lengths per title is production work. Deciding what the book *is* stays with their marketer.

## 5 · The partnership framing — state the exchange, do not dress it

Paul wants the partner relationship present, and there is an honest way to put it that is stronger than the polite way.

**We are not proposing a bespoke build, and not proposing they adopt something finished.** Both would be untrue. The honest description is an **exchange**: they get infrastructure shaped around how a publishing house actually runs, at a stage early enough that the shaping is real. We get something we cannot buy — sustained access to professionals who know what a publishing operation requires.

**State that plainly rather than dressing it as a discount.** It is the part of the arrangement most valuable to us, and a buyer who spots an undisclosed benefit thinks worse of the document than one who was told.

Then convert "we do not presume to know your business" from a disclaimer into a **structure** — a short table of open questions, each with who answers it:

| Open question | Who answers |
|---|---|
| What does your handoff to Hachette actually require, and in what form? | Production |
| Do your designers work in their own tools and hand us files, or would they work in ours? | Their design lead |
| What does a target date mean in your house — handoff, publication, or both? | Them |
| Where does a notes package sit in your editorial process, and who signs it? | Their editors |

**None is a blocker.** The system runs without them and is better with them, and that difference is roughly the difference between a tool and infrastructure.
