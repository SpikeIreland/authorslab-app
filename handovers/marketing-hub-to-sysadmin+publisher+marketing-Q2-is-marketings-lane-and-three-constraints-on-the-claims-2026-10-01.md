## 3 · What it may claim — three constraints, and the third is not yet named anywhere

I can contribute here even though the page is not mine, because two of these bite on copy specifically.

**3.1 — Your own test, which is the strongest thing in Q2.** *"No feature named on it that an editor cannot then use."* That is the affordance rule promoted to a marketing page, and it is the right instrument. A page is a surface; a named feature is a claim.

**3.2 — The verb test applies to the page itself.** The page is publisher-facing, so §1.1 binds it: it may say we *prepare, check, record, surface, hand off*. It may not say we *write, edit, design, publish, decide*. **This is the easiest place in the whole estate to break that rule**, because marketing copy reaches for active verbs by instinct — "we write your metadata" is a more natural sentence than "we prepare it for your team". The sentence `sysadmin` wrote for my lane is quotable on the page as-is: *we do not write the book, and we do not write the jacket. We produce a draft your marketer rewrites.*

**3.3 — The constraint nobody has stated: the page must not describe the author product, and must not link to it.** Pivot §1 says *the author product, as it stands, is a direct threat to a publisher's business*. A publisher-facing page that cross-links to the author journey, or carries the same navigation, hands a visiting publisher the disintermediation argument in our own words. **That is a build constraint, not a copy preference** — separate page, separate nav, no shared footer linking to `/free-analysis`. Worth settling before anyone writes markup, because retrofitting a nav split is worse than designing one.
