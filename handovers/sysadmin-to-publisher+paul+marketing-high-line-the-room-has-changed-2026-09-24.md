## 2 · What the entity actually is

The July research describes "a new Jewish imprint that Neil is backing," with Jacky setting it up across the UK and US. That was accurate for July. It has since launched, and it is bigger than an imprint:

**High Line Publishing Studio** — "a new international publishing studio for independent voices and original thinking." New York and London. Founded by **Neil Blair**, backed by a coalition of publishing, media and philanthropic investors. **Distributed globally by Hachette.**

Two imprints:

| Imprint | Remit | Executive Publisher |
|---|---|---|
| **Odessa Editions** | Jewish and Israeli fiction and non-fiction | **Jacky Klein** |
| **Antidote Books** | "Bold, cross-spectrum non-fiction" | Joel Simons |

Others named: **Oliver Malcolm, CEO** · Lindsay Gordon, SVP · Aviya Kushner, Senior Editor (Odessa) · **Lee Child**, Executive Committee.

So the org chart is: Neil Blair founded it → Oliver Malcolm runs it → Jacky runs one of its two imprints.

---

## 3 · Who Oliver Malcolm is

20+ years in UK trade publishing, across sales, strategy, editorial and leadership.

- **HarperCollins, from 2010.** Divisional Sales Director (Fiction) → Publishing Strategy Director → **Executive Publisher (2019)**, overseeing HarperNonFiction, Avon and HarperNorth. He **founded HarperNorth** in 2020, the Manchester-based division. Avon won Imprint of the Year at the 2020 British Book Awards.
- **Hodder & Stoughton, Oct 2022.** Managing Director, and a member of the **Hachette UK board**. Left after under two years (Dec 2024).
- **Oliver Malcolm Publishing Transformation Ltd**, incorporated 21 Oct 2024. A consultancy helping "publishers, literary agents, and authors achieve meaningful transformation in an industry facing unprecedented change." Offers free support to neurodiverse individuals.
- **Now CEO of High Line.**

Read that CV as a shape, not a list. He is a **commercial operator who builds new things inside big houses** — he started an imprint from scratch in Manchester, took a legacy imprint's MD seat, then left to sell transformation as a service, then took the top job at a startup publisher. He is not a caretaker.

**His own framing, from High Line's site:**

> "High Line is built on a simple principle: we exist to serve the author — not the other way around. From day one, we are being completely open about who we are and how we work... By keeping our mission focused, we can therefore invest where it matters most: discovering exceptional books, **giving authors the editorial attention they deserve**, and connecting them with readers right around the world."

That last clause is our pitch in his words. Mirror it.

## 4 · Five things this changes about the demo

**4.1 · The economic buyer moved.** Jacky is the *user*; Malcolm is the *buyer*. He is purchasing infrastructure for two imprints and a shared studio, not tooling for one list. Carl should be reading the room for two people with different questions, not one.

**4.2 · "Imprint-in-a-box" now undersells.** For Jacky it was the right frame. For Malcolm the frame is **studio infrastructure** — one editorial spine serving Odessa and Antidote, with a portfolio view across both. **This promotes the Publisher Portal from a nice-to-have to the centre of the Malcolm track.** `publisher`: your surface is now load-bearing in a way it wasn't 24 hours ago.

**4.3 · The "drop the AI-safety framing" call is now risky.** The July doc reasons — correctly, for Jacky — that her 2023 lecture calling AI "transformative" means we can skip the human-in-the-loop, IP-safe, no-training-on-your-manuscripts material. **Do not extend that inference to Malcolm.** Ex-HarperCollins Executive Publisher and ex-Hachette UK board is precisely the seat where AI licensing and rights exposure are institutional questions, and a man who sells publishing transformation professionally will have a considered position. Recommendation: **don't lead with it, but have it loaded.** Assume he may test us.

**4.4 · He will ask operational questions the aesthetic pitch does not answer.** Cost per title. Throughput. What headcount this replaces or frees. Where the human stays. How it affects a P&L on a small list. The July doc's advice — that Jacky will judge us aesthetically, so lean on Design and typography — remains true *for Jacky*, and is not sufficient for him. Carl needs either numbers or a clean "we'd size that with you," and should not improvise a figure. Note Paul's standing position: leave the room without disclosing a price to a publisher.

**4.5 · The Blair downstream is no longer downstream.** July treated Neil Blair as a hoped-for onward introduction via Mark Forstater. He is the **founder of the company in the room**. The Script → Brontë bridge is a much shorter walk than the doc assumes — but that also means a loose claim gets to Neil faster.

## 5 · Two facts to hold carefully

**Hachette distributes them globally.** Malcolm sat on the Hachette UK board. Do not propose anything that reads as cutting across that relationship.

**Odessa's remit is broader than "Jewish imprint."** In Jacky's words: fiction and non-fiction, established and emerging, "secular and religious perspectives, from across the political spectrum, and from all around the world." Some books engage directly with Jewish life, Israel, history or antisemitism; "others will be celebratory, imaginative, personal, or unexpected." The July doc's instruction — treat the mission seriously, don't frame it as an addressable demographic — holds and strengthens.
