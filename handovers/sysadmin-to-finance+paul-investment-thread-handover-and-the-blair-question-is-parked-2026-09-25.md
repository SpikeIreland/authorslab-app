## 2 · What broke in the August pack — one sentence

From `docs/sis/pricing/AuthorsLab-Executive-Summary-V1.pdf` (10 Aug, pre-seed £350k, SEIS £250k + EIS £100k):

> "A separate imprint SKU (**£250–£1,000/mo**) opens the B2B axis — small UK imprints demonstrably pay £600/mo for editorial SaaS today, and our September demo is to the Executive Publisher of a new London imprint backed by The Blair Partnership, with a 20–50 author list."

**The footnote ate the thesis.** The B2B axis was an adjacent SKU capped at a thousand a month, and the Blair meeting appeared as *evidence the footnote might work*. It worked. A CEO wanting a system embedded in his organisation is not a £1,000/mo subscriber, and that old ceiling is now nearer the floor.

Four structural changes, not cosmetic ones:

| | August pack | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of value | a subscription seat | a publishing house's production line |
| Revenue quality | prosumer SaaS — CAC-sensitive, churn-exposed | enterprise — reference-sold, low churn, expands in-account |
| Why-now | author incomes falling, fragmented tools (**consumer**) | publishing is under-resourced and AI-anxious, nobody built the ops layer (**industry**) |
| Traction register | 11 beta authors · 272 chapters | a Neil Blair-backed publisher wants it embedded |
| Valuation basis | ARPU × subscribers vs ~£32k MRR projection | contract value, pipeline, defensibility |

The founders' story gets **stronger**, not weaker: Carl's publishing-insider credibility matters more in an industry sale than a consumer one.

The new narrative spine already exists — `docs/pitch/2026-09-05-industrial-methodology-onepager.md`, *"everyone else asks the buyer to trust a mind; we show them a machine."* It is aimed at publishers; it wants re-aiming at investors. Nothing needs inventing.

## 3 · Your two jobs, in order

**3.1 — Publisher pricing. This one is live and blocking a real proposal.**

We have three author tiers and effectively nothing for publishers. The **shape is already ruled** (engagement brief §5) and it is a positioning decision, not a modelling output:

- **Per-seat is wrong.** It punishes a three-person imprint with large ambitions for being small and rewards it for staying small — backwards for a startup publisher, and Oliver will feel it.
- **Per-title plus a platform fee** bills him for the thing he told us he wants: books reaching market. It scales with his success rather than his headcount, and Paul's stated objective is *an easy decision* — one line beats a matrix.

Model within that shape. If the shape is wrong, argue it with evidence — but argue it before you model, not after.

**Do not model until scope lands.** `identity-billing` is building the org/imprint/membership model and `publisher` the journey on top of it. A model built on a guessed scope is a number nobody can defend in front of a man who ran Hodder.

**3.2 — The investor narrative and model refresh. Second, and gated on 3.1.**

`AL-Financial-Model-V0.5.1.xlsx` is built on the consumer funnel. It needs a B2B line that is no longer a rounding error. Do not rewrite the author economics — they still hold and they are instrumented, which remains genuinely unusual. Add the enterprise motion beside them.

## 4 · RULING from Paul, 2026-09-25 — the Blair investment question is PARKED

The thought that arose: if AuthorsLab becomes the first publishing AI portal, The Blair Partnership might want to get behind it and push it into the market.

**Paul's decision: not now, possibly never. Build for maximum utility to a publisher first.**

I raised three hazards and he accepted the second as decisive. Recorded here so the thinking exists if it ever revives, and so nobody re-derives it from scratch:

1. **Related-party pricing.** Our first and anchor enterprise contract would be with a company our investor owns — he would sit on both sides of the number that sets our valuation. Structurable, but before a negotiation, not during.
2. **Channel contamination.** Publishing is small and territorial. Would Hachette or PRH adopt an operations system part-owned by a literary agency? Note High Line is *distributed by Hachette*, so they are already adjacent. **This is the one Paul ruled on.**
3. **Timing.** Raising now prices the round on a promise; raising after six months of Highline running live prices it on proof. Our own doctrine, applied to the cap table: creation is a claim, execution is a state.

**What this means for you operationally:** do not build an investment narrative that shapes the roadmap. A deck that needs a feature to exist is how products get bent toward a fundraise. The order is **customer utility → evidence → narrative**, and never the reverse. If you find yourself wanting a product decision to make a slide work, that is the signal to stop and courier it.

Not parked: the Brontë/Script angle. Neil Blair co-founded Brontë Film and TV with J.K. Rowling and it is producing the HBO Harry Potter adaptation; a manuscript-to-screen pipeline is a second thesis sitting beside publishing ops. Worth knowing, not worth chasing.
