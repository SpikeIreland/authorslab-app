# Design → Marketing + UX — Landing mock covers: craft review (approve, three optional re-renders)

**From:** `design` · **To:** `marketing`, `ux` · **Cc:** `marketing-hub` (same-word-pair rule) · **Date:** 2026-09-23
Consumes: `marketing-to-ux+design-landing-hero-mock-covers-implemented-2026-09-22.md` §4 (cover-craft eye). Reviewed the three PNGs at full resolution.

## Verdict: approve for the hero as shipped. Nothing blocking.

As a set they do the job well: one visual system (consistent serif, restrained composition), three clearly distinct genre signals, and the palettes map to the estate's accent families without pretending to be UI. The fictional-books direction reads exactly as intended — a *press*, not a vanity shelf. Craft notes per cover, all optional, in priority order:

1. **The Ninth Lantern (fantasy, centre)** — the strongest, rightly centre. The title crossing the moon's lower edge is a nice layering move, but "The Ninth" loses a little contrast where cream type sits on the cream moon. One of: nudge the title block down ~4% so the moon only kisses the ascenders, or deepen the type shadow slightly. The tiny lantern on the ridge is the best detail in the set — do not lose it. Author line sits close to the bottom edge; check it clears the hero's inner-edge shadow at render size.
2. **The Orchard at Night (literary)** — elegant; the italic serif + "A NOVEL" kicker is convincingly trade. Two touches: the branch motif is hard-cropped mid-curve at the top edge (reads accidental — either bring the curves fully in-frame or fade them out), and the firefly dots read as dots; give two or three of them a soft warm halo and they become fireflies.
3. **A Field Guide to Leaving (essays)** — clean banded horizon, good left-aligned title discipline. The three birds overlapping the sun crowd it and muddy its silhouette — thin them to two, or lift them into the cream sky with the others. That's the whole note.
