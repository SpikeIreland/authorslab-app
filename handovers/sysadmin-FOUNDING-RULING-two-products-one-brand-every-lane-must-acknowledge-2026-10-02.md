## 1 · The model, stated once, plainly

> **AuthorsLab is two discrete products. Their only shared surface is the brand identity.**

**The author product** — a *lite* system. An individual author's personal publishing tools.

**The publisher product** — a *Pro* system. A publishing house's working environment.

**They never meet.** Not in navigation, not in messaging, not in data a user can see. There is no crossover journey, no shared screen, no handoff between them.

### Shared below the waterline, invisible above it

One engine. One Craft Call Cell. One Alex/Sam/Jordan lineage. One schema. **"Never meet" is a statement about experience, not architecture.** Two applications of one engine — and neither kind of user can tell.

This is why R8 ruled voice as a *parameter* rather than duplicate prompt sets: one engine, two audiences.

### What follows, and these are not optional readings

- **A publisher's authors are not platform users.** The notes package leaves as a **deliverable** — a document sent to an author. There is no in-platform handoff and no cross-product messaging seam to build.
- **Every book on a publisher's list is house-ingested.** The entitlement model collapses to one case.
- **The author product is not a lesser publisher product**, and the publisher product is not the author product with more features. They are different products for different buyers.

### The single carve-out

Proposal V0.12 promises Oliver that *"cover concepts can be shared with authors for comment, and a record shows who approved what."* That is the one two-way author touchpoint in anything we have sold. It is **carved out and open**, pending `design` and `publisher`. Everything else in §1 stands.

## 4 · The process failure, and the fix

**A decision Paul states in chat reaches one lane. It must reach the estate.**

**RULED:** when Paul states a model, a decision or a ruling in any chat, **the lane that hears it couriers it that same turn** — to all lanes, not to the lane that seems relevant. `ux` did this unprompted today and it is the only reason the model moved at all. Attribution stays with Paul; the lane is the scribe, not the author.

A lane that is unsure whether something was a decision should courier it as *"Paul said this to me, recording it so the estate has it"* and let it be contested. **The cost of over-couriering a decision is a paragraph. The cost of under-couriering one is four days of lanes building the wrong product.**

## 5 · And a second fix, because this was unauditable

Delete-on-read means nobody — including Paul — can prove after the fact whether something was delivered. When he asked me this morning whether delivery was broken, **I got the answer wrong on first measurement** because an absent pointer means either "never sent" or "read and correctly deleted", and the filesystem cannot distinguish them.

**PROPOSED** for the House Rules bump: consumed pointers are **moved to `inbox/<slug>/read/`, not deleted.** Same empty-inbox discipline, same signal, but delivery becomes provable. A read-receipt that destroys itself is not a receipt.
