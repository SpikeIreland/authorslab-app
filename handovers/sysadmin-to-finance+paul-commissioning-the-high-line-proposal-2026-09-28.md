## 4 · What the proposal must contain

Seven sections. Short. He is in startup mode with a great deal to do.

**4.1 · His problem, in his words.** Open by quoting him: *"We have taken on a lot of new authors and we need to get them to market as soon as possible."* Then show we understood it better than he stated it — this is a **throughput** problem, and the question he actually needs answered every week is *which book is going to slip*.

**4.2 · High Line specifically.** Two imprints, Odessa under Jacky and Antidote under Joel, UK and US, Hachette distribution, first books spring 2027. Named correctly and briefly. It demonstrates listening, and it is the cheapest credibility in the document.

**4.3 · What the system does about it.** The production line, the Lobby that answers *what is late*, the portal as the drill-down when he asks *why*. Written as capability, not as a feature list.

**4.4 · What is live today, and what we build.** The honesty section — §5. This is the section that wins or loses it.

**4.5 · How he adopts it without betting the company.** Observe → Assist → Operate, per module, **switches held by his admin, not by us.** The line to land: *at level one the system has no power to be wrong about anything except its own reporting.* He is being asked to trust nothing at the start, and to advance each module when he chooses.

**4.6 · What it costs.** Per-title plus platform fee. Ruled, and the reasoning is yours to express: per-seat would punish a three-person imprint for being small and reward it for staying small, which is backwards for a publisher trying to grow a list. He pays for books reaching market — the thing he told us he wants.

**4.7 · What happens next.** One decision, not five. An easy yes is a **small** yes.

## 5 · What it must not claim — the affordance rule at document level

A proposal is a document made almost entirely of claims. The single thing that could cost us Oliver is one sentence describing something that does not exist.

**Required artefact: a SAY / DON'T-SAY-YET table**, per Founding Principles P10, built before the prose and binding on it. Live things in present tense, in-flight things in roadmap tense, trajectories as trajectories, numbers dated.

Hard rules:

- **Nothing in the present tense that is not live in production.** Not merged, not built — live.
- **No named capability without a substrate.** The affordance rule applies to a sentence exactly as it applies to a button. We removed controls last week for this; we do not reinstate them as prose.
- **Do not echo "embedded in the organisation" back as a capability.** It is *his* phrase and it implies integration with what they already run, Hachette distribution, contracts, royalties, UK/US data residency. We have built none of that. Using his words to describe our product is the most natural overclaim in the document and the easiest to make by accident.
- **No number that is not instrument-backed.** Our unit economics are genuinely measured per author, per manuscript, per feature — that is unusual and worth saying. It is only worth saying while every figure is real.
- **No competitor named. No claim that the AI understands or judges.** We sell a machine, not a mind.
- **No date we have not planned backwards from.** A slipped date in the first proposal, to a man whose problem is slipping dates, is not recoverable.

## 7 · Before it goes — the verification pass

Paul's words: *we only get one chance*. So this does not ship on a proofread.

1. **Every claim traced.** Each sentence asserting a capability points at a production artefact or sits in roadmap tense. Not a spot-check — the whole document, and you state the population: *N claims, M traced, 0 untraced.*
2. **`publisher` countersigns the scope section**, executed rather than reviewed — they open the surfaces and confirm the description matches.
3. **One adversarial read.** Someone reads it as Oliver would: hunting the gap, pressing the button, asking what happens when it breaks. I will do this if you want a lane that has not been holding the pen.
4. **Paul and Carl own the send.** Nothing goes without both.
